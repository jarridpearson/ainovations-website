Your notices, where residents will actually see them.

You are required to publish your meetings, hearings, road closures and bids. You do, and you pay for it — in a newspaper almost nobody in town still reads. Towncrier is the part that was missing: one place residents can search, on the phone they already carry.

The requirement is met. The purpose isn't.

Publication rules were written when the newspaper was how a town reached everyone. That stopped being true a long time ago, and nothing replaced it. The notice runs, the invoice is paid, the file is clean — and the first a resident hears about the rezoning is when the survey stakes go in.

Nobody sees it

A legal notice runs once, in small type, in a paper with a fraction of the town subscribed.

Nobody can find it later

"What did the board decide in March?" means a phone call to the clerk, or a trip to the office.

The clerk gets the blame

You did everything the statute asked. Residents still turn up angry that they weren't told.

How it works

Four steps, and only the second one is your job.

Prove the town is yours to speak for

You confirm control of the municipality's own domain, and we check the officials page on your own website. The confirmation code goes to the contact printed there — not to an address typed into a form. Nobody speaks for your town by filling in a signup.

Post the notice once

Plain language, a category, and a date. Attach the agenda PDF. Schedule it for a Monday morning if you like. It takes about ninety seconds.

Residents get it

It appears on your town's page, in search, and as a notification for anyone following the town — who never has to make an account to do so.

You can show the board what happened

Every notice carries a count of how many residents actually opened it. When you ask for next year's line item, you have a number instead of a feeling.

What it will not do

Towncrier supplements legally required publication. It does not replace it. Keep publishing exactly as your statute requires. Nothing here satisfies a statutory duty, and any product that told you otherwise would be putting your hearings at risk of challenge. Every notice names the publication the legal version ran in.

Towncrier is not an emergency notification system. There is deliberately no emergency category. Keep your county alert service for life safety — that is what it is built and contracted for, and this is not.

Pricing

One fee for the municipality, billed annually because that is how a budget line works. No per-notice charge and no per-resident charge — residents never pay and never sign up.

If you ever stop paying, everything you have already published stays public and stays searchable. A notice of a public hearing does not stop having been given because a purchase order was late.

Residents never sign up

No account, no email address, no phone number. Someone opens the app or the web page, finds their town, and follows it. The list of who follows your town is never shared with the municipality — a record of who is watching the council is not something we will hand over, and not something a town should want to hold.
