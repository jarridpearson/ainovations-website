Do You Own Your Website If You Rent It?

Short answer, and we would rather you hear it from us than find out later: on a rented website you do not own the build. We build it, we host it, we operate it, and that is exactly what keeps the monthly price where it is. If owning the files outright matters more to you than the monthly rate, renting is the wrong product and you should buy a site instead.

That is the whole honest version. Everything below is the detail most providers leave you to discover on your way out.

What you keep, no matter what

  • Your content. The words, the photos, the pages — if you cancel, we export it and hand it to you. It was always yours.
  • Your domain name. We register it, renew it and pay for it as part of the service, so you never have to think about it. If you ever want it in your own account — you are leaving, you hired someone else, or you simply want it in your name — we transfer it and charge only the actual cost of the transfer. No markup, no release fee, no holding it hostage.
  • Your business information. Your prices, services, hours and copy are yours to change, and you can change them yourself.

The domain point is worth sitting on, because it is where people actually get hurt. A domain registered in a provider's name, by a provider who later decides to be difficult, is how a business loses the web address its customers have been typing for ten years. We will not do that to you, and you can hold us to this paragraph.

What you are actually buying

You are buying the thing working, every month, without you. The build is included — there is no setup fee on any Rent-a-Site plan, ever — and that is only possible because the arrangement runs on a term rather than a one-off payment. You are not paying for a folder of files. You are paying so that the hosting, the monitoring, the updates and the monthly work are somebody else's problem.

Put the other way: a bought website is an asset you then have to maintain. A rented one is a service that maintains itself. Neither is dishonest — they are different purchases, and the mistake is buying one while expecting the other.

The term is a real commitment

Rent-a-Site is sold on a one-year or three-year agreement. It is not month-to-month and we will not pretend otherwise. The three-year term exists for one reason: it is cheaper per month. That is the only difference between the two.

If you want to know what leaving early costs, what happens at the end of a term, or whether the price can move, those specifics are in the agreement rather than on this page — ask us at support@ainovations.net and we will put them in front of you before you sign anything, not after.

When you should buy instead of rent

Buy outright if any of these are true:

  • You have someone in-house who will genuinely keep the site maintained.
  • You want the codebase itself as an asset — you are raising money, or selling the business, and the website is part of the package.
  • You need to build something unusual enough that a managed plan would be fighting you.

None of those describe most five-person shops. Most five-person shops want a site that is alive, current and not their problem, and that is the case renting actually wins on.

The question to ask any provider

Not "do I own it?" — everyone has a comfortable answer to that one. Ask: "If I cancel tomorrow, what do I walk away with, and what does it cost me to take my domain?" If the answer is vague, or if the domain costs anything beyond the registrar's own transfer fee, you have learned something useful.
