What we build

Six things built for a body shop.

Not a five-page brochure with a phone number on it. These are the things your customers are trying to do — and right now every one of them turns into a phone call you have to take.

They send you photos of the damage THE BIG ONE

  • Six photos off their phone, their plate number, and three questions: can you drive it, did the airbags go off, is anything leaking?
  • They get a ballpark range and a time you'll call them back
  • It lands on your phone as a text with the pictures attached

We opened six body shop websites around here. Not one of them lets a customer send a photo. Somebody standing in a parking lot looking at a caved-in door has the same one option on every single site: call, and hope somebody picks up.

“You can pick your own shop” THE ONLY QUESTION THEY HAVE

  • A plain page telling them what most people don't know: the insurance company can suggest a shop, but they can't make you use it.
  • Your DMV shop number, right there, so they know you're legitimate
  • And the sentence that closes it: we deal with the adjuster and the paperwork, you don't

This is the one thing every collision customer wonders and nobody answers. Not one shop site in the region has written it down. It also brings you people searching for exactly that.

They check on their car without calling KILLS THE CALLS

  • They put in their last name and plate and see where it's at: torn down, waiting on parts, in paint, going back together, ready
  • With a date, and a couple of real pictures you texted in from the floor
  • That's four “is it done yet” calls a car your front desk doesn't have to take

Owners light up when they see this one. It doesn't bring you a single new customer. It just gives your office person their day back.

Before and afters, added by text YOUR BEST SALESMAN

  • You text two photos to a number. They show up on the site. That's the whole job.
  • No logging in. No password. No uploading anything.

A quarter panel on a Silverado, before and after, sells harder than every word on your website put together. The reason most shops don't have a gallery isn't that they don't have photos — it's that adding them is a pain. So we made it a text message.

What you're certified in, up top WHY YOU, NOT THEM

  • I-CAR, your manufacturer certifications, years in business, your written warranty
  • Whether you do the camera and sensor calibration in house — more cars need it every year, and if you do it and the shop down the road farms it out, that's worth saying out loud

Most shops have real credentials sitting in a drawer and nothing about them anywhere a customer can see.

If you tow: the impound page FOR COMBINED SHOPS

  • Their car got towed. Where is it, what are the gate hours, what's the daily charge
  • Exactly what to bring — registration, ID, insurance card — and what you take for payment
  • Plus a one-tap tow request that sends your driver a map pin

This is the busiest phone call in the towing business and nobody around here has put it on a page. If you run trucks and a lot, this one alone is worth the money.

Questions

The things you're actually thinking

I'm booked six weeks out. Why do I need this?
You don't need more cars. What you might need is better cars — customer-pay work instead of whatever the insurance company sends. And you definitely don't need your front desk taking four calls a car about whether it's done. That's what this is for. If your bays are full and your phone is quiet, don't buy this.
Most of my work comes from the insurance company anyway.
For now. Ask yourself what percentage of your work is customer-pay today versus two years ago. If that number is going the wrong way, you've got maybe a year before you find out nobody outside that pipeline can find you. Shops that are getting out from under the insurance programs are the ones who need this first.
I already pay somebody for a website.
Probably one of the big phone-sales companies, a few hundred a month, six-month minimum. Tell us what you pay and when it's up. If ours costs less and does more, switch when your term ends — we'll build yours first and flip it the day the old one dies. Worth checking whether you can even log into the one you have now. Most shops can't.
Can you really give somebody a price off a photo?
No, and we'd never build it that way. A photo gets them a range and a callback time — nothing more, and the page says exactly that. You still write the real estimate after you put hands on the car. What it does is stop somebody with a wrecked car from calling four shops and going with whoever answered first.
Who keeps it up to date? I don't have time.
We do. The gallery you feed by texting photos — that's it, that's the whole job on your end. Everything else is ours: keeping it online, keeping it safe, putting something new on it, keeping your Google listing right.
Can you get me to the top of Google?
No. Anybody who promises you that is lying. What we do is build a site that loads fast, that Google can actually read, with your towns and your certifications spelled out, and put something new on it every month. If you're starting from a Facebook page or a dead site, that alone makes a real difference.
I do towing and storage too. Does this cover that?
Yes, and it's one of the better reasons to do it. The tow request goes straight to your driver's phone with a map pin, and the impound page answers the busiest call you take — where's my car, what do I owe, what do I bring. Nobody around here has that page.
What if I want out?
The site comes down, we hand you your content and your domain name, and that's that. We don't hold anything hostage — which is more than you can say for how you probably got your current one.
