The four things that decide compatibility

1. Water type & parameters

This is the dealbreaker most beginners miss. A fish bred for soft, acidic water (most tetras, rams) will slowly fail in the hard, alkaline water African cichlids need — and vice versa. Match temperature, pH range, and hardness first; nothing else matters if the water's wrong.

2. Adult size

That cute 1-inch fish at the store is a juvenile. Check the adult size and the tank it needs. A common pleco hits 18 inches; a bala shark needs a 6-foot tank. If a tankmate can fit another fish in its mouth when both are grown, assume it eventually will.

3. Temperament

Peaceful, semi-aggressive, and aggressive fish mix badly. Fin-nippers (tiger barbs, some tetras) shred long-finned fish like bettas and angelfish. Territorial fish need space and sight breaks. Schooling fish kept in too-small groups get stressed and nippy themselves — keep them in groups of 6+.

4. Bioload & stocking level

Every fish adds waste. Overstock and ammonia and nitrate climb faster than your filter and water changes can keep up — which stresses everyone and invites disease. Compatible fish in an overstocked tank still fail. Leave headroom.

Quick compatibility red flags

  • Mixing a known fin-nipper with any long-finned or slow fish.
  • One fish that needs hard/alkaline water and another that needs soft/acidic.
  • A predator whose mouth is bigger than a tankmate's body.
  • Two males of a territorial species in a tank with no room to escape.
  • Adding anything to a tank that isn't fully cycled yet.
