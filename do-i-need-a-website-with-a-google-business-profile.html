Do I Need a Website If I Have a Google Business Profile?

Short answer: yes — because a Google Business Profile is a listing, not a website, and it isn't yours. Your profile lives on Google's real estate, follows Google's rules, and can be edited (or suspended) without your permission. A website is the one piece of your online presence you actually own. You want both, and they do different jobs.

What a Google Business Profile does well

Don't skip the profile — it's free and it's how you show up on the map. A well-kept profile:

  • Puts you in the local "map pack" when someone searches your trade near you
  • Shows your hours, phone, directions, photos, and reviews at a glance
  • Is often the first thing a nearby customer sees on their phone

If you don't have one yet, claim it today. It is the single highest-return free thing a local business can do.

What a profile can't do (that a website can)

  • You don't own it. Google can change the layout, hide fields, or suspend the listing. Your website answers to you.
  • It only ranks for "you." A profile helps people who already know your name or trade + town. A real site can rank for the dozens of specific questions your customers actually type.
  • There's no room to sell. Services, pricing, FAQs, before-and-after galleries, booking, an app for repeat customers — none of that fits in a listing.
  • It can't build trust the way a site does. When a stranger is deciding whether to call, "no website" quietly reads as "smaller / riskier." A clean site closes that gap.
  • It can't feed the rest of your marketing. Ads, email, social, and business cards all need a home to point to. A profile can't be that home.

The honest verdict

Use both, on purpose. The profile wins you the map pack; the website wins everyone who wants to look closer before they buy. They reinforce each other — a linked, active website even helps your profile rank. The mistake isn't having a profile; it's stopping there and hoping a listing does a website's job.
