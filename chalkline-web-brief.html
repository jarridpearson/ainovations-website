Careful · do not mention needing a payment method

Do not write “no card needed” — it is false. But do not write “a card is required to start” either. Both versions have been on this page and both were wrong for different reasons: the first lied, and the second announced something every buyer already assumes, which reads as either nervous or condescending and pushes the price down the page. State the price and the billing period and stop. The live fine print is the model: “Billed monthly through Stripe. Cancel any time from inside the portal.”

One rule holds throughout: Chalkline never takes a percentage of a contractor’s invoices. That is a real product fact and it is the sharpest thing the page has to say. Say it as the bill does not move with revenue — invoice $50,000 or $500,000 and the monthly figure is identical. Do not reach for card-processing percentages to make the point: the 2.9% a processor charges is paid either way, by every contractor on every platform, so quoting it as a saving is wrong and an earlier draft of this page made exactly that mistake. The honest version is that the contractor connects their own Stripe, Square or PayPal, the money never passes through AInovations, and their processing rate is never marked up.

Careful · what “bank transfer” actually is

Chalkline lists a fourth payment option beside those three, and it is not a fourth processor. Choosing it lets the contractor type how they want to be paid — an e-transfer address, bank details, where to post a cheque — and that text prints on every invoice. Nothing is processed, nothing reconciles automatically; they record the payment by hand when the money arrives. Do not write “pay straight into your bank account” or list it alongside Stripe as though it were an equivalent rail. It is worth mentioning — plenty of contractors are paid by cheque and e-transfer — but as get paid however you already get paid, not as a payment integration.

The product

What Chalkline is

Job management software for small residential construction companies — the paperwork side of a contracting business, built to be used with gloves on and one hand free.

It is two apps sharing one account. A phone app for the jobsite, and a web portal for the desk.

Who it is for

Framing, siding, decking, roofing and general residential contractors running a crew of roughly two to ten. The design target was a specific shop: six people, four concurrent jobs, framing and remodelling and siding. It was built alongside a working framing & siding contractor, which is worth saying on the site — it is true and it is rare.

The problem it solves

A contractor quotes off the tailgate, and then loses the evening to paperwork. Chalkline moves the quote, the invoice, and the crew's hours onto the phone that is already in their pocket, so the job is priced before they leave the driveway.

Jobs & photos

  • Jobsite photos, private to the company by default
  • Progress reports a client can be sent

The desk (web portal)

  • Timesheets, job costing, schedule board
  • Full CSV export of everything, any time

Four ways a contractor gets paid · zero platform cut

A contractor connects their own Stripe, Square or PayPal account, or simply states bank-transfer terms that print on every invoice. Their customer pays them directly into their own merchant account. Chalkline never holds the money and takes no percentage.

This matters for site copy: it is the clearest differentiator against the field-service platforms that skim a percentage.

What was deliberately left out

The existing page has a section on this and it should survive any redesign — saying what a tool refuses to do is more persuasive than another feature list. There is no GPS tracking of crew, no percentage of invoices, and no attempt to be an accounting package.

Voice

How Chalkline talks

Plain, concrete, unsentimental. Short sentences. Trade vocabulary used correctly — courses, squares, stringers, o.c. Never startup register, never “empower,” never “seamless.”

“Quote it. Schedule it. Build it. Bill it.”

“Four ways to get paid. Zero platform cut.”

“Hours, not whereabouts.”

“The math you argue about.”

Those first two are live on the current page and are approved. The last two are the store screenshot headlines and are equally usable.

Accessibility note

Chalk blue #5aa2ff is a dark-ground accent. On white it fails contrast for body text — darken to roughly #0f56b3 for links and small text on light surfaces, and keep #5aa2ff for large type, rules and fills. Same for teal: #1dc8c6 on light needs about #07767b.

The three legal pages are served by the portal app at those paths, and both app stores hold live links to them. If the marketing site takes over the domain or the routing, those exact paths must keep resolving or the store listings break. Worth confirming with whoever owns DNS before launch.

Pages the site needs

  • Home — the pitch, the four-ways-to-get-paid section, what was left out, and one primary call to action (which CTA depends on the open decision above)
  • Pricing — only once the decision is made
  • Sign in — links straight to the portal, not a form on the marketing site
  • Contact — a real person answers; the current page says so and it lands well
  • Existing privacy, support, delete-account — leave alone

There is no self-serve signup on the phone apps by design: a company owner creates the account in the portal, then adds their crew. The site should point owners to the portal and not imply crew can sign themselves up.
