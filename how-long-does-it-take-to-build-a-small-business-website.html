How Long Does It Take to Build a Small Business Website?

Honest answer: the building is fast. The waiting is what takes weeks. A simple, professional small-business website is a few days of actual work. Most projects that drag on for a month or two aren't slow because the site is hard to build — they're slow because of decisions, content, and back-and-forth. Here's where the time really goes, and how to shrink it.

The three common paths, roughly timed

  • DIY builder (Wix, Squarespace, etc.): a weekend to "live," but realistically a few weekends before it looks like something you'd hand a customer. The build is free-ish; your time isn't.
  • Freelancer or agency: two to eight weeks is typical for a small site. Most of that is scheduling, revisions, and waiting on each other — not coding.
  • Managed / done-for-you: days, because someone builds it for you from a proven starting point and you just approve it. This is the model our Rent-a-Site program runs on.

What actually eats the time

  • Content. Photos, your list of services, hours, and the words on each page. This is the #1 thing that stalls a website — it waits on you, not the builder.
  • Decisions. Colors, layout, "can we move that up." Small choices, big calendars.
  • Domain & email setup. Pointing your domain and getting professional email working can add days if it's not handled for you.
  • Review rounds. Every "one more change, then it's perfect" adds a scheduling gap on both sides.

How to be live faster

The single biggest lever is starting from a proven design instead of a blank page, and having one person own the whole thing — build, domain, email, hosting — so nothing waits in a hand-off. Gather your logo, a handful of photos, your services, and your hours before you start, and you remove the delay that stalls most projects.
