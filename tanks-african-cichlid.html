African Cichlid Tank: The Freshwater Reef Full of Color and Personality

If you've ever stood in front of a reef tank and wished you could have that kind of electric color without the salt, an African cichlid tank is the answer a lot of hobbyists never hear about. Rift Lake cichlids from Lake Malawi and Lake Tanganyika come in blues, yellows, oranges, and stripes that genuinely rival marine fish, and they do it in hard, alkaline freshwater. Add in the fact that these fish have real personality, defending territories, digging, sparring, courting, and you've got one of the most rewarding tanks in the hobby. It's also one of the easiest to get wrong. Let's set you up to get it right.

What Makes an African Cichlid Tank Different

The whole game with Rift Lake cichlids is recreating the water they evolved in. These lakes are ancient, mineral-rich, and rock-strewn, and the fish expect that at home.

Water is non-negotiable. African cichlids need hard, alkaline conditions. Aim for these cichlid water parameters:

  • pH: roughly 7.8 to 8.6
  • General hardness (GH): 12 to 20 dGH
  • Carbonate hardness (KH): 10 to 18 dKH
  • Temperature: 76 to 82°F
  • Ammonia and nitrite: 0, always, in a fully cycled tank

That high KH is your friend. It buffers the pH and keeps it stable, which matters because these fish do poorly with swings. If your tap water is soft or acidic, you'll want a proper cichlid mineral buffer or a crushed-coral / aragonite substrate to hold those numbers steady.

Go bigger on footprint. These are active, territorial fish. A longer, wider tank gives you more floor space to break up sight lines and spread out territories. Length and footprint matter more than sheer height for cichlids.

Rock is the aquascape. Stack stacked slate, lava rock, or Texas holey rock into caves and crevices. Each fish claims a zone, and the rockwork is what defines those boundaries. Build it on the bottom glass or an eggcrate base, not on top of loose substrate that fish can dig out from under.

Use controlled overstocking, carefully. This sounds backwards, but a moderately crowded cichlid tank is often more peaceful than a sparse one. When there are lots of fish, no single fish can lock down a territory and relentlessly chase one target, aggression gets diffused across the group. This only works with strong filtration and a disciplined water-change routine, because more fish means more waste.

Know your groups. Mbuna are the rock-dwelling, algae-grazing bruisers of Lake Malawi, colorful and feisty, and they eat a largely vegetable diet. Peacocks (Aulonocara) and haps (Haplochromines) are generally more open-water, more carnivorous or omnivorous, and often less relentlessly aggressive but bigger. Many experienced keepers don't mix mbuna with peacocks and haps, the diet needs differ and the mbuna tend to bully the calmer species. Pick a lane per tank.

Careful with tank mates and plants. Peaceful community fish, tetras, gouramis, most barbs, get bullied or eaten. Robust companions like Synodontis catfish are a classic, cichlid-tough choice. And herbivorous mbuna will shred soft plants; stick to tough species like anubias and java fern anchored to rock, or go with a clean rock-only scape.

FAQ

How many African cichlids can I keep together?

More than you'd expect. Controlled overstocking is a legitimate strategy, a fuller tank spreads out aggression so no one fish gets relentlessly targeted. It only works with strong filtration and consistent water changes to handle the extra waste. Start with a larger-footprint tank and stock a cohesive group rather than a few random fish.

Can I mix mbuna with peacocks and haps?

Most experienced keepers don't. Mbuna are aggressive rock-grazers on a veggie-heavy diet, while peacocks and haps are calmer, larger, and more carnivorous. The diet mismatch and the bullying usually make it a bad combo. Build one tank around mbuna and another around peacocks/haps.

What fish can live with African cichlids?

Robust, cichlid-tough tank mates. Synodontis catfish are the go-to. Skip peaceful community fish like tetras and gouramis, they get bullied or eaten. TankMate's compatibility engine will flag risky pairings before you commit.

Do I need special water for a cichlid tank?

Usually, yes, unless your tap is already hard and alkaline. Target pH around 7.8 to 8.6, GH 12 to 20 dGH, and KH 10 to 18 dKH. A crushed-coral or aragonite substrate and a cichlid buffer help hold those numbers steady. Log it in TankMate to keep the Tank Health Score in range.

Can I keep live plants with mbuna?

Only tough ones. Herbivorous mbuna will destroy soft plants, so stick to anubias and java fern anchored to rock, or embrace a rock-only aquascape. TankMate warns you if a plant you add is likely to become a snack.
