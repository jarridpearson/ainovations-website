How Much Does a Website Cost for a Small Business?

Ask around and you'll get every answer from "free" to "five grand" — and confusingly, they're all true. What a small business website costs depends entirely on who does the work and who keeps it alive afterward.

The usual ways to get a website

  • A designer or agency: commonly $1,500–$5,000+ up front for a small business site — and that's the build alone. Hosting, updates, and changes are extra, forever.
  • DIY builders (Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy): $16–$50 a month — but you do all the work. For most owners the site gets half-built once and never touched again, which is why so many DIY sites are quietly embarrassing.
  • A friend or relative who "does computers": cheap until they get busy — and then nobody can update your site at all.

The cost nobody budgets for: keeping it alive

The build is the beginning, not the end. Hosting, security updates, content changes, search-engine basics, analytics — a website that nobody maintains is a website that slowly stops working for you. The upkeep, not the build, is where most small business websites die.
