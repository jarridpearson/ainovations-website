Questions

The things owners and artists actually ask.

Why 1% on top of the monthly?
The monthly keeps your app running and covers your two hours. The 1% is the part that only costs you something when the app is bringing money in — we make more when you make more. $3 on a $300 tattoo, $350 on a $35,000 month. It's on the client's receipt and on your reports. And it's on tips too, one rule for everything, because the alternative is somebody charging $1 for the work and $300 for the tip to dodge it.
Where does the money go?
Into your own Stripe account the moment it's paid. Stripe takes its normal processing and sends it to your bank on its normal schedule — about two business days out, and the first payout takes about a week. We take 1%. We never hold your money, and we can't log in to your Stripe — we see the payments your app sends through it, which is how the 1% is figured and the reports get built. Artists aren't on Stripe — the app tracks what each one earned and tipped, and you pay them out of your account with a statement that does the math. That goes for booth-rent artists too: their sessions land in the studio's Stripe, the statement takes the rent off, and you pay them the rest.
How do deposits work?
They're charged when the client books, not held. Non-refundable, applied to the final, forfeited on a no-show or a cancel inside your window — you set the amounts and the window per artist and per type of booking, and the policy is on the screen before the client pays. For no-show fees, the client's card is saved and they agree to the policy at booking, which is what the card networks want to see when there's a dispute. A dispute lands on your Stripe account, like any card charge you take today — the app keeps the policy they agreed to and the receipt on file, and if the client wins, the money comes back out of your account.
What if a client ghosts after the drawing?
They can't get a drawing without a deposit. The custom-piece flow doesn't let the artist upload the drawing until the deposit is paid and the request has become a booking. If they ghost after that, the deposit's already yours.
Can my artists post their own work?
Yes — that's the point. On Studio and Collective every artist has their own login: their page, their portfolio, their books open or closed, their calendar, their requests, their money. They post from the chair and send it to Instagram from the same screen, and finish the post there. You see everything; they see theirs.
What can't go in the app?
Nudity — anywhere in the app: portfolio, flash, reference photos, messages. Apple and Google won't publish an app with it, and we won't build one. Everything else is yours to post. Work you can't show in the app, keep in the shop's book and show in person.
Does the consent form actually meet New York's rules?
Yes. New York requires that before every tattoo the client and the artist both sign a form confirming the sealed single-use needles were opened in front of the client, and that you keep it seven years. Your app's form does exactly that, digitally, on the client's phone, and archives it. Under-18 is blocked at the date of birth — New York allows no parental consent for tattoos. Electronic signatures are valid under state law. Other states differ; the form and how long it's kept are set per studio with New York's as the floor. Your studio still needs its own Health Department permit — the app doesn't give you one or stand in for it.
What about Instagram?
Your artists post to the app first and send it to Instagram from the same screen — it opens in Instagram and they finish the post there. Instagram stays where people find you; the app is where the work lives and where they book, so you never run a book out of DMs again. Pulling the Instagram posts you pick into the app is next, not in yet — it needs a one-time sign-off from Meta on our end, not yours, and there's no date on it. Until then, your artists put the pieces they want in the app up themselves.

How many texts do I get?
1,000 a month on Solo, 2,500 on Studio, 6,000 on Collective — reminders count. They don't carry over. Go past your number and it's 2¢ a text, and we'll tell you before you get there.
What happens if I cancel?
You keep the store listing under your name, your Apple and Google accounts, your Stripe, and your client list — consent forms and point balances included. Take it with you. What stops is what we run inside the app: booking, payments through the app, the request flow, reminders, loyalty, the back office, the assistant — so the app stops working the day the agreement ends. Your agreement runs its term and renews for the same length unless you tell us in writing 60 days ahead.

They'll want a D-U-N-S number — free, and we start it on day one. Nothing goes in the store until you've approved it.
What do I actually have to do?
Sit with us for the Apple and Google sign-up — it has to be in your business's name. Connect your Stripe. Set your deposit rules and your cancellation policy, once. Tell your artists to log in and post. Say yes to the campaigns we write. That's the list.
