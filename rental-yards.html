What we build

Six things, built for a rental yard.

Most of it is work you've already done. We're reformatting it, not inventing it — which is why this is the fastest build we do.

Your rate sheet, readable with a thumb THE BIG ONE

  • Four-hour, day, week and month — the same columns you keep now
  • They type “skid steer” and get it, instead of scrolling a table sideways
  • You change a rate from your phone and it's live

If you already maintain a rate sheet, this is mostly done. We take the numbers you have and put them somewhere a man in a truck can actually read them.

“Will my truck pull it?” NOBODY HAS THIS

  • Operating weight, width, and what trailer and hitch it needs, on every machine
  • A plain line that says whether a half-ton will do it or he's bringing something bigger

This is the question behind half your calls, and the reason for a share of your damage. Answer it on the page and the guy shows up with the right truck.

Reserve this date — as a request HOW IT HAS TO WORK

  • He picks the machine and the days and sends it
  • It hits your phone as a request, clearly marked not confirmed
  • You confirm or you don't — and he's told that up front

We build it this way because it's the only honest way. It still captures the job at nine at night when you're not answering, and it never puts you on the hook for a machine that hasn't come back yet.

What delivery costs, before he asks SAVES THE CALL

  • Your delivery and pickup charge by town, published
  • How far out you'll go, and where you won't

Right now somebody has to stop and work that out on the phone, several times a day, for jobs half of which never happen.

Photos that actually show up SOUNDS SMALL, ISN'T

  • Every machine, visible on a phone without tapping or hovering anything
  • Your real yard and your real iron — not stock pictures of somebody else's fleet

A renter wants to see the machine before he commits to a day rate. If the picture doesn't load, he assumes you don't have it.

Yard status, two taps KILLS THE CALLS

  • “Both skid steers out until Thursday” — you post it from the phone in your pocket
  • Date-stamped at the top of the page, so nobody wonders if it's stale

The most-asked question you get is whether you've got one right now. Answering it once on the page beats answering it thirty times a week.

Questions

The things you're actually thinking

I already publish my rates. What am I paying you for?
Then you're ahead of nearly everyone, and this is the cheapest build we do — we're not creating the hard part, we're moving it. What you're paying for is that your rates become readable on a phone, searchable, and attached to photos and tow specs, instead of sitting in a table that was built for a desktop monitor. Same numbers. Somewhere they can actually be used.
I can't have a website telling people a machine is available.
You're right, and we wouldn't build it that way. Machines come back late and they come back broken. Every reserve button is a request that lands on your phone, labeled not-confirmed on both ends, and you approve it or you don't. Nothing on the site ever commits your iron.
I take cash in advance. I'm not taking cards online.
Then don't. That's why we tell you to stay on Growth and skip Commerce — it's $300 a month you shouldn't spend. Your terms stay exactly what they are; the site just stops making people call to find out what things cost.
My site's fine. It's been up for years.
Open it on your own phone and try to read a rate, then try to see a picture of a machine. That's the whole test. A lot of these sites were built before phones mattered and they have no mobile setting at all — which doesn't look dated so much as it makes the page unusable at the exact moment somebody's deciding.
Who's going to keep it up to date? I'm running the yard.
We do. You change a rate or post “both skid steers out until Thursday” from your phone in a few seconds. Everything else is ours — keeping it online, keeping it safe, putting something new on it, your Google listing. If we need something from you, it's one text.
Can you get me to the top of Google?
No. Anybody who promises that is lying. What we can say is that the out-of-state page farms beating local yards right now are winning on published prices and pages built for phones — both of which are things we do, and neither of which is a ranking promise.
I rent, but I also sell and service. Does this still fit?
Yes, and that's a common shape around here. The rate sheet and reserve requests cover the rental side, and the service side gets a request form and a plain statement of what you're backed up on. Tell us the real mix and we build it that way around.
What if I want out?
The site comes down, we hand you your content and your domain name, and that's that. Nothing gets held hostage.
