Why "just go viral" is a hope and not a plan

A viral moment is something that happens to you. You can't schedule it, repeat it on demand, or bank on it. Plenty of people have had a video do enormous numbers and made nothing from it, because views are not income — views are attention passing through, and attention leaves as fast as it arrives.

The other problem is that none of it is yours. The followers live on a platform you don't own, reached through an algorithm that changes without telling you. A platform can change what it shows tomorrow and your reach is cut in half through no fault of your own. That's not a risk you can manage. It's a landlord.

The people who actually make a living at this are boring about it. They post consistently for years, not weeks. They build something they own — an email list, a real site, a body of work — so a bad month on one platform isn't a bad month in their bank account. And they make themselves easy for a brand to check and easy to pay.

That last part is the only bit we can help with. It's also the part almost nobody does.

Questions

The ones you're actually thinking

Linktree is free. Why would I pay $99?
If all you need is somewhere to park links, use the free one — genuinely, don't pay us. You'd pay us when you want the thing a link page can't do: a media kit a brand can evaluate, a domain with your name on it, an email list you own, and someone whose job it is to keep it current. That's a different product that happens to also hold links.
Can you get me more followers?
No. We have no idea how to do that for you and neither does anyone charging you for it. Followers come from making things people want to watch, over and over, for longer than feels reasonable. We handle the part that happens after that.
Will this get me brand deals?
Not on its own. What it does is remove a reason to pass on you. When a brand looks you up and finds a real site with your numbers, your audience and your past work laid out, you look like someone who's done this before. That doesn't create the deal — it stops you losing one you'd otherwise have gotten.
I've got 2,000 followers. Am I too small?
Possibly, and we'd rather say so. Small and engaged is a real business — plenty of brands prefer it. Small and inactive isn't, yet. The honest test is whether anyone would notice if you stopped posting. If the answer is no, spend the $99 on better gear or better ideas and come back to us.
How fast does it pay for itself?
We don't know, and anyone who gives you a number is guessing. One brand deal usually covers a year of this, but we can't tell you when or whether you'll get one. If you need this to pay for itself in month one, don't buy it.
What if I stop posting?
Then the site goes stale and it won't save you — nothing we build works if there's nothing behind it. Cancel and we'll hand over your domain and your list with no argument. We'd rather you leave cleanly than pay us for something that isn't working.
Who actually makes the changes?
We do. You send a text or an email with what's new — updated numbers, a new sponsor, a new rate — and it goes up. That's one hour a month of our time, which for most creators is more than enough.
Do you take a cut of what I earn?
No. Not of brand deals, not of merch, not of anything. $99 a month is the entire amount you pay us.
