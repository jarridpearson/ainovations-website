Status, not just points

Regular, VIP, Legend. VIPs get the secret menu and hear about specials first. Legends get their name on the wall and a seat on Friday night. You decide the perks. A coupon gets used once. A title gets defended.

Challenges and streaks

All twelve wing flavors. Four Tuesdays straight. Double points on your dead night. Something on their birthday. Bring a friend, you both eat better.

Rewards they redeem in seconds

A reward is a code. They show it, your server punches it into Toast. Ordering in the app? They type it at checkout. One use per phone number — Toast enforces it at the register and at online checkout. No new hardware. One code to punch in.

A pass on their lock screen

Their card sits in Apple Wallet or Google Wallet like a boarding pass. Points update on it. Get near your place and it can pop up on the lock screen. Your name on their phone, even with the app closed.

Questions

The things owners actually ask.

Can my customers order in the app?
Yes. They tap Order and your Toast online ordering opens right there in the app. Toast takes the money, prints the ticket, handles delivery — same as today. None of that touches us, and Toast doesn't have to sign off on anything. What the app does is everything around the order: knowing who your regulars are, bringing them back, and making your app the place they order instead of DoorDash.
How does the loyalty program know when someone orders?
You give us a read-only look at your Toast. Takes a few minutes, nothing for Toast to approve. When somebody orders in the app with the phone number or email they signed up with, points land, usually within a couple of minutes. When they eat in, they tap once or scan the card on the table. DoorDash and Uber Eats orders get nothing. That's on purpose — points are for coming to you.
Why would a customer order in my app instead of on DoorDash?
Because they get something for it. And it can be cheaper. A lot of places bump their prices on the delivery apps to cover the fee — if you do, the same food from the same kitchen costs less in your app. Then give them points that only count when they order from you, a secret menu, and a VIP title they don't want to lose. The regulars come back to you.
How is this different from Toast's own app?
Toast's is a template — their screens, your logo and colors, points-per-dollar loyalty — for $299 a month, plus $185 a month if you want texting. Ours is your app. Your pages, your features, VIP tiers and a secret menu, alerts that know the weather and the game, a card in their Wallet, memberships your regulars buy from you, a list of names you own, and somebody running all of it every month. Both check out through your Toast online ordering — theirs inside Toast's template, ours inside your app.
Do I need Toast?
Yes — Online Ordering, on Toast's Restaurant Management Essentials suite or higher. Essentials is what lets us see your orders; Online Ordering is what runs the Order button. If you've got both, you're set. On something else? Tell us anyway and we'll let you know when we get there.
Do you take a cut of memberships, tickets, or merch?
No. Never. Everything you sell in the app goes through your own Stripe account into your bank. Stripe takes its normal processing, we take nothing. What you pay us is the monthly, the setup, and 2¢ a text if you go over your plan. That's it. Apple, Google, and Stripe get paid by you, and it's all spelled out under the plans.
Who owns the app?
You do. It's in the stores under your name, your Stripe is yours, your list of regulars is yours — take it any time, including the day you leave. The delivery apps own your customers. We built this so you do.
Is there a contract?
Yes. One year or three. Go three and we waive the setup fee — $1,000 on Regulars, $1,500 on Members, $2,500 on Group. When your term's up it renews for the same length — three years rolls into three more — unless you tell us in writing 60 days before it ends.
How many texts do I get?
1,000 a month on Regulars, 2,000 on Members, 5,000 on Group. Hard cap, doesn't carry over. Go past it and it's 2¢ a text — and we'll tell you before you get there.
What happens if I cancel?
You keep the app, your Apple and Google accounts, your Stripe, and your list — points and all, so you can honor them however you want. Take it with you. The app stays on their phones and the Order button still works, because that's your Toast. What stops is the stuff we run: the loyalty, the texts, the alerts, the reports. Your agreement runs its term and renews for the same length unless you tell us in writing 60 days ahead.
What's the card in the bag?
A little card that goes in every takeout and delivery bag — including the ones DoorDash picks up. It says: download the app, order here next time, get points. It lands in their hands the exact moment they're happy with your food. We write it, design it, put your QR code on it, and send you the file. You print them and drop them in the bags.
How long until it's live?
The slow part is Apple and Google. Getting your business set up with them and the app through their review usually takes four to six weeks for an LLC or corporation, and we do that paperwork with you. They'll want a D-U-N-S number — free, and we'll get it started on day one. Toast has no say in it at any point.
What do I actually have to do?
Give us the read-only look at your Toast, once. Sit with us for the Apple and Google sign-up — it has to be in your business's name. Punch the promo codes we write into Toast. Put the counter sign up, drop the cards in the bags, and have your crew say the line: download the app, sign up, 10% off today — or whatever you want the deal to be. Say yes to the campaigns we write. That's the list. We build the app, set up the loyalty, make the sign and the cards and the posts, write every text and alert, and send you one report a month.
