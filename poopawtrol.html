Behind the counter

The half of the app the customer never sees.

A booking app is only as good as the tools the owner gets. PooPawtrol ships with a private back office that runs the entire business from a phone browser — the schedule, the money, the customers and the settings.

Everything in the build

  • Native apps for iPhone and Android — one codebase, published to both stores under our developer accounts, with the store listings, icons, screenshots and privacy policy written and submitted.
  • A booking engine that understands the road — it only offers times that fit, using real drive time between stops plus a buffer she sets, job lengths she sets per service, and an allowance for extra dogs.
  • One-time and repeating plans — weekly, twice a week, every two weeks and monthly, each holding its slot and rolling forward on its own.
  • Payments — Venmo, card, or cash at the visit, with saved payment methods and automatic charging on each visit day for repeating plans.
  • Failed-payment handling — a decline puts the plan on hold, tells her why, and waits for her instead of silently retrying or losing the customer.
  • A month calendar and day stepper — visit counts on every day, arrows through any day past or future, and a tap to jump to that day's run.
  • Time off that asks first — block a day or a few hours and, if visits are already booked in it, the portal lists them and offers to move them all, each re-timed on one page and saved together, or to leave them alone.
  • Her own hours and settings — opening hours per weekday, one-off hours for a single date, job durations per service, buffer time, and extra minutes per additional dog.
  • Calendar sync — connect once and every visit is copied across within seconds, and stays correct when something is booked, moved or cancelled.
  • Push notifications — she writes a message in the portal and it goes to every customer who opted in, with a delivery count and a log of what was sent.
  • Four automatic emails — new booking and cancellation alerts to her, a nightly list of tomorrow's visits, a night-before reminder to each customer, and a "your visit has moved" notice with the old and new times.
  • A secure owner login — private, forced password change on first sign-in, and a password she can change any time.
  • Hosting, updates and monitoring — the back end, the database, the scheduled jobs and the store releases are all ours to run. She never patches anything.
