## 2. Cold email — local businesses (for /hire)

Target: businesses within an hour of you with a visible manual-process problem
(dispatchers, clinics, property managers, marinas, farm suppliers, gyms).
Send 5 a day. Subject lines short and specific.

**Subject:** Quick question about [Company]'s scheduling / paperwork

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm a software developer in [your town] — I build apps and internal tools
> for small businesses (my own apps are on Google Play and the web:
> ainovations.net/hire).
>
> I'm reaching out because [one specific observation — "I noticed your booking
> is still phone-only" / "a friend mentioned your crew still does paper
> timesheets"]. That's usually a sign a few hours a week are going to work a
> small tool could do.
>
> If there's a task at [Company] everyone hates doing, I'd be glad to take a
> look and tell you honestly whether software would help and what it would
> cost. The look is free either way.
>
> — Jarrid Pearson
> AInovations LLC · jp@ainovations.net · ainovations.net/hire

**Follow-up (5–7 days later, reply to the same thread):**

> Hi [Name] — floating this back up. If the answer is "not now," that's
> completely fine and I'll leave you be. If there's a better person at
> [Company] to ask, I'd appreciate a point in their direction.

## 3. Contractor outreach (for /chalkline)

Target: framing, siding, roofing, remodeling companies with 1–10 people.
Where they are: local Facebook contractor/trades groups, supplier bulletin
boards, and your design partner's network.

### The ask to your design partner (most important single message)

> Now that you're running on Chalkline, would you introduce me to two or three
> other contractors you respect? It's free for them during early access, and
> every company that joins makes the product better for you too. A text like
> "my software guy built this for my company, worth a look:
> ainovations.net/chalkline" is all it takes.

### Facebook group / forum post

> I build software, and last year I built a tool with a framing & siding
> contractor who was drowning in paperwork: quotes, invoices, job schedule,
> crew clock-in/out, and getting paid — all in one place.
>
> A few things we did on purpose that trades folks seem to appreciate:
> no GPS tracking of the crew, no percentage skimmed off your invoices
> (payments go straight to YOUR account), and your data exports any time.
> QuickBooks stays — this runs the field side.
>
> It's free while in early access (no card, no trial countdown) because I'd
> rather have feedback from real crews than money right now:
> https://ainovations.net/chalkline
>
> I'm the developer, so if it's missing something your company needs, tell me
> and there's a decent chance I build it.

### Direct email/DM to a specific contractor

> Hi [Name] — I build software for small construction companies. My design
> partner runs her framing/siding company on it: quotes, invoices, jobs,
> schedule, crew time, payments straight to her bank with no cut taken.
>
> It's free during early access and takes about two minutes to set up:
> ainovations.net/chalkline
>
> If you try it and it doesn't fit how you work, I genuinely want to know why —
> that feedback is worth more to me than the signup.

## 5. Weekly rhythm (the whole plan on one line each)

- **Mon–Fri:** 5 cold emails (mix of local-business and contractor lists) — 20 min
- **Tue:** one LinkedIn post (a build note, a before/after, a lesson) — 15 min
- **Wed:** one post/comment in a trades Facebook group — 10 min
- **Fri:** follow-ups on everything older than 5 days — 15 min
- **Always:** answer /hire form inquiries within one business day (the page promises it)

Volume math: ~25 messages/week ≈ 100/month. At even a 3–5% conversation rate
that's 3–5 real conversations a month — one decent freelance project a month
is the $60K/yr pace all by itself.
