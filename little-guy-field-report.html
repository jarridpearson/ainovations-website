From the Field

Why We Back the Little Guy

One night. Four stores. Two basic questions. Only one store's answers wouldn't have risked the fish.

TankMate · Field report · 4 min read

TankMate partners with independent fish shops, not the big-box chains. People ask us why. So we ran a simple test: we walked into four stores in one night with real tank questions and wrote down exactly what we were told. Three chains sent us home with advice that ranges from useless to dangerous. One local shop got it right in under a minute. Here is what happened.

Four stops, one honest scorecard

Two ordinary questions, the kind any new fishkeeper asks: how to raise a tank's hardness (KH), and how long to wait before adding fish to a brand-new tank. We asked the first at both Petcos and the second at PetSmart and the local shop. Everything below is what the staff actually said, with the correct answer next to it, and what TankMate returned when we asked it the same thing on the spot.

1 PetcoBig-box chain · Stop one Didn't know
We asked

How do I raise the hardness in my tank — specifically the KH?

We heard

Two staff, including the one introduced as their fish expert, said they didn't know how. The first told us he didn't even know what KH was. After a quick search on her phone, the expert read back an answer — the same one TankMate had already given us.

KH is carbonate hardness — the water's buffering, what keeps pH stable. It's day-one knowledge on a floor that sells fish. TankMate said: raise KH with a carbonate buffer or crushed coral, with target numbers for the fish in question.
2 PetcoBig-box chain · Stop two Wrong product
We asked

Same question — how do I bring the hardness up?

We heard

We were told to buy a water-conditioning product. When we searched it in the app and on Google, both said the same thing: it has no effect on hardness at all. When we pointed that out, the reply was, "Well, you should just get fish that like softer water."

A water conditioner removes chlorine. It does not change hardness. Selling it as a hardness fix is wrong, and "pick different fish" dodges the question we came in with. TankMate said: that product won't move GH or KH — use a mineral or carbonate supplement dosed to a target, not a dechlorinator.
3 PetSmartBig-box chain · Stop three Dangerous advice
We asked

Brand-new tank, fresh water, hardness reading 0 and 0. How long should I wait before adding fish?

We heard

"As early as 24 hours after the fresh water goes in." We asked about the bacteria. They said the bacteria grow right when the fish need them, and 24 hours is the standard rule for a new aquarium. We didn't buy fish — we gave a short lesson on cycling and showed what the app said. The reply: "Well, we recommend 24 hours and have really good luck with it."

This is how new fish die. A new tank has no nitrifying bacteria yet. They take weeks to establish, not a day. Add fish at 24 hours and ammonia and nitrite climb with nothing to process them — new-tank syndrome. Hardness and KH reading 0 is its own warning: no buffer, so pH can crash. TankMate said: cycle the tank first, build the biological filter, and wait weeks — not hours — before adding livestock.
★ Ack's Exotic PetsIndependent shop · Cicero, NY Right call
We asked

Same new-tank question: how long before I can add life?

We heard

Instantly, they flagged the hardness as a problem and said it needed to come up for the fish we were considering. They guided us to a proper hardness fix — one that matched a TankMate recommendation. Then they told us to wait two to four weeks on a brand-new tank to grow the biological system that keeps it healthy, and walked us through what to do and when.

Correct on every point — and no agenda behind it. The only money made was the hardness fix we actually needed. No unnecessary livestock put at risk, no upsell. Just what the tank required. Matched TankMate on: the hardness problem, the right fix, and a multi-week cycle before adding life.

This is our partner model

We are not against big stores. We are for good advice. On this night, the independent shop had more knowledge, higher expectations for the hobby, and guidance that protects the animal instead of endangering it. That is the pattern we keep seeing, and it is why TankMate builds its partner program around local shops.
