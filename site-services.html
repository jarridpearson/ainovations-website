What we build

Six things built for the work you actually do.

Not a cookie-cutter site with your name dropped in. This is the stuff your customers try to do — and right now, every one of them ends up as a phone call to you instead.

Your prices, right on the page THE BIG ONE

  • What a pump-out costs by tank size. What digging up a buried lid costs. Extra hose. Nights and weekends. All of it, in writing.
  • Every price has a date next to it — "prices as of February 2." So it's a number, not a promise you're stuck with.
  • You change any price yourself, on your phone, in about twenty seconds. You don't call us and you don't wait.

We looked at every septic and dumpster outfit we could find around here. Not one of them puts a price on their website. They all say "call for a free quote" — and that's why your phone never stops.

People pick a week, not a time FITS HOW YOU RUN

  • They pick a week. Not 2:15 on Thursday, which would wreck the rest of your day.
  • They get a text the night before, and you call when you're thirty minutes out.
  • They tell you the stuff you actually need up front: gate code, is there a dog, where to park, is the lid buried — and they can send a photo.

Most booking systems make people pick an exact time. You'd hate it and you'd stop using it in a week. This one works the way you already run — by what town you're in, not by the clock.

We text them when they're due again THEY COME BACK

  • A homeowner answers four easy questions and finds out roughly when their tank needs doing.
  • They can ask for one text when that month comes around. Not junk mail. One message.
  • So a one-time job turns into a customer who calls you again in three years instead of calling whoever's first on Google.

This is about as close to steady money as this work gets. It runs on its own and it costs you nothing.

They can book a toilet or a dumpster themselves THE RENTAL SIDE

  • Having a party? They put in how many people and how long, and the page tells them how many toilets they need.
  • Dumpsters described how people actually think: "a kitchen and a bath," "a two-car garage cleanout." Not cubic yards.
  • How much weight is included, and what it costs if they go over — before they book.

A surprise weight bill two weeks later is the thing people complain about most in this business. Putting the number up front costs you nothing and ends the argument before it starts.

A page just for real estate agents THEY COME BACK TOO

  • One price for a closing inspection, how fast they get the report, and it goes to the agent, the buyer and the bank all at once.
  • Built so an agent saves it — because they'll need you again in about three weeks.

Banks ask for a septic inspection on a lot of house sales. Agents send the same guy over and over. Nobody around here has a page aimed at them.

Your insurance certificate, ready to grab WINS JOBS

  • A contractor can pull your COI off the site at ten at night without calling you.
  • If he needs to be added as additional insured, that request comes through the site instead of your voicemail.

Small thing. Wins jobs. Costs you nothing to have sitting there.

Questions

The things you're actually thinking

I'm booked three weeks out. Why would I want more calls?
You wouldn't. We're not selling that. We're selling fewer calls. Prices on the page stop the "how much?" calls. The booking page stops the phone tag. The reminder brings back people who already used you. If you end up busier, it'll be because the days run better — not because we dumped a pile of leads on you that you can't get to.
I already pay somebody for a website.
Tell us what you pay and when the contract is up. A lot of guys around here pay about $255 a month for a site with no prices on it. If ours costs less and does more, switch when your contract ends. We'll build the new one first and flip it over the day the old one dies.
I don't want my prices where my competition can see them.
Fair. We hear it every time. Two things. One, they already know what you charge — your customers tell them. Two, that's why every price has a date on it. "Prices as of February 2" is a number, not a handcuff, and you can change it on your phone in twenty seconds. Meanwhile the guy who won't call without seeing a price is walking right now.
Who's going to keep it up to date? I don't have time.
We do. Prices you change yourself in seconds. Everything else is ours — keeping it online, keeping it safe, putting something new on it, keeping your Google listing right. That's what you're paying for. If we ever need something from you, it's one text message, not homework.
What if I want out?
The site comes down, we hand you your content and your domain name, and that's that. We don't hold anything hostage.
Can you get me to the top of Google?
No. And anybody who promises you that is lying. Here's what we actually do: build a site that loads fast, that Google can read properly, with your towns and your services spelled out, and put something new on it every month. If you're starting from a Facebook page or a dead link, that by itself makes a real difference. But nobody can promise you a spot.
I do more than septic. Does that matter?
No — most guys run three or four of these off the same phone. Septic, porta-potties, dumpsters, digging, land clearing, fence, junk hauling. It all goes on the one site, and the prices and the booking work the same way for every one of them.
