Why Is My Aquarium Water Cloudy?

The fix depends on the color. Milky-white, green, and brown cloudiness have completely different causes — treat the wrong one and you'll make it worse. Here's how to tell them apart and clear it for good.

TankMate · Water quality · 6 min read

White / milky water — a bacterial bloom

By far the most common, especially in new tanks. A sudden explosion of free-floating bacteria clouds the water grey-white. It's usually triggered by a new tank still cycling, overfeeding, or disturbing the substrate.

Fix: don't panic and don't do giant water changes — that just feeds the cycle. In a new tank, let it finish cycling; the bloom clears on its own in a few days to two weeks. Feed lightly, don't add more fish, and make sure your filter media isn't being over-cleaned. If it's an established tank, it usually means something decomposing (uneaten food, a dead fish, a buried snail) — find and remove it.

Green water — an algae bloom

A pea-soup green tint is free-floating algae, fueled by too much light and too many nutrients (nitrate and phosphate). Direct sunlight on the tank is a frequent culprit.

Fix: cut the lighting to 6–8 hours a day, move the tank out of direct sun, stop overfeeding, and bring nutrients down with water changes. For a stubborn bloom, a UV sterilizer clears it fast. Getting your nitrogen cycle under control prevents it coming back.

Brown / yellow water — tannins

New driftwood leaches tannins that stain the water like weak tea. It's harmless — actually beneficial for many soft-water fish — but not everyone wants the look.

Fix: it fades over weeks. Speed it up by pre-soaking or boiling new wood, running activated carbon, and doing regular water changes.

When cloudy water is actually dangerous

Cloudiness itself rarely hurts fish — but the cause can. If fish are gasping at the surface, clamped, or lethargic alongside the cloud, stop guessing and test the water for ammonia and nitrite. In a new tank, a bloom plus stressed fish usually means the tank isn't cycled and ammonia is rising.
