Why Isn't My Business Showing Up on Google?

You searched your own trade and your own town, and back came three competitors, a directory you have never heard of, and a shop that closed in 2021. Not you. It is a genuinely maddening thing to discover, and most of the time it is fixable without paying anybody.

First, work out which problem you have

Search your business name, exactly as it is on the sign. Then search what a stranger would type — "plumber near me," "auto body Watertown," "excavating contractor." Those two searches test completely different things, and the fixes have nothing to do with each other.

  • You come up for your name, but not for the job. Google knows you exist. It just does not think you are one of the answers. That is a ranking problem.
  • You do not come up even for your own name. That is an existence problem, and it is the easier of the two to fix.

The usual causes, roughly in order of how often we find them

  • No Google Business Profile, or one you never claimed. Google often creates a listing for a business it has heard of. If nobody claimed it, nobody is maintaining it, and it ranks like the abandoned thing it is.
  • A profile with the bones missing. No categories, no hours, no service area, no photos. Category in particular does a lot of work: picking the wrong one quietly removes you from the search you care about.
  • Nowhere to send anybody. Google prefers to rank businesses it can hand a visitor off to. No website is a real handicap, and it compounds — see what a profile does and does not cover.
  • A site with no words on it. Beautiful hero image, logo, a phone number in a graphic, and nothing Google can actually read. Text in an image is invisible to search.
  • No reviews, or none in two years. Review count and recency both matter, and "we have great customers, they just never post" does not register.
  • Your name, address and phone disagree across the web. Old suite number on one directory, a disconnected landline on another, "& Sons" on a third. Each inconsistency makes Google a little less sure you are one business.

What you can fix yourself, today, for nothing

Claim the Google Business Profile — it is free, and it is the single highest-return hour in small business marketing. Set the primary category to the thing you actually want to be called for. Fill in hours, including holiday hours. Set the service area if you travel to customers. Put up ten real photos of real work, not stock. Then ask five recent customers for a review, by name, in person or by text. Not a campaign. Five.

That alone moves a lot of businesses from invisible to findable, and if it does, you are done and you do not need us.

Where it stops being free

What the free hour cannot do is win a competitive "near me" search against shops that have a real site with real pages about the work. At that point you are not fixing a listing, you are building the thing the listing points at — which is a different job with a different price. Worth knowing what that actually costs before anyone quotes you.

Be wary of anyone who answers "why am I not showing up" with a monthly SEO retainer before they have looked at your profile. The free fixes come first. Anybody skipping them is selling, not diagnosing.
