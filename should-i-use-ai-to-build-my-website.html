Should I Use AI to Build My Website?

We build with AI every day, so read this as a practitioner's take rather than a defense of billable hours. The short version: AI is genuinely good at getting a website made, and genuinely bad at the part that comes after. Whether that matters depends entirely on who is going to look after it.

What it is actually good at now

  • First drafts. Give it your trade and your town and it will produce serviceable page copy in seconds. Better than a blank page, and usually better than what a busy owner writes at 11pm.
  • Layout and structure. It knows what a services page looks like. You will not get something broken.
  • The tedious correctness. Alt text, meta descriptions, heading structure, a sitemap — the unglamorous things people skip, done consistently.
  • Rewriting your worst paragraph. Paste it in, ask for shorter and plainer. This works well and costs nothing.

Where it falls down, and these are not small

  • Facts about your business. It will confidently invent your hours, your service area, your prices and your years in business. It is not lying; it is filling a gap with something plausible. Every concrete claim on an AI-written page needs checking against reality before it goes live.
  • Anything with legal weight. Licensing claims, insurance, warranty language, privacy policies, accessibility statements. Generated text here is a liability wearing a nice font.
  • Anything that needs an account. Connecting a domain, email records, a payment processor, a booking system, analytics. This is where most DIY builds stall — not at design, at DNS.
  • Knowing what to leave out. AI writes more. Most small business sites are losing to having too much on them, not too little.

The part nobody warns you about

A website is not a thing you make. It is a thing you keep. AI gets you to launch day impressively fast and then has no opinion about the next three years: it does not renew your domain, patch the platform, notice the site is down on a Saturday, update your holiday hours, or tell you the contact form stopped delivering six weeks ago.

That is not a knock on AI. It is the same gap that swallows sites built by a person who then moved on — which is its own quiet disaster. The build was never the expensive part.

When you should absolutely just use AI and skip us

Honestly: if you are comfortable with technology, you enjoy this sort of thing, and you will still be logging in next year — use the AI builder. You will get a decent site for very little money, and hiring anyone would be wasting it. That is a real answer and we would rather give it than take money for a thing you did not need.

Hire somebody when the opposite is true: when the site needs to be alive and correct in two years and nobody in the building is going to do it. At that point you are not buying a build, you are buying the maintenance — and the sensible thing is to compare it against what a DIY builder really costs over time.
