DIY Website Builder vs. a Managed Website

Let us start where most comparisons will not: a DIY website builder is a genuinely good product, and for some businesses it is the right answer. We sell the other thing, and that is still true.

So the useful question is not which is better. It is which one fits the person who has to live with it.

What a builder is actually good at

  • It is cheap to start, and you can see something on screen in an afternoon.
  • You can change a price or a photo at eleven at night without asking anyone.
  • For a simple brochure site run by someone who enjoys fiddling with it, that is a complete solution.

If that describes you, use one. We mean that — you do not need us.

Where it tends to come apart

Not in month one. In month seven.

  • The site stops changing. The hours on it are last year's hours. The photo is the truck you sold. Nobody decided to stop maintaining it; everyone just got busy.
  • The "afternoon" was not an afternoon. The template fights you, the phone layout breaks, and the thing you wanted takes four evenings you did not plan to spend.
  • Nobody is watching it. If the contact form silently stops delivering, a builder will not tell you. You find out when a customer says they emailed twice.
  • The boring technical work goes undone. Page titles, descriptions, a sitemap, your business details marked up so machines can read them. A builder gives you the fields; it does not fill them in well.
  • You are still the webmaster. That was the job you were trying not to have.

What managed changes

The build is done for you and you approve it before anything goes live. After that the maintenance is not a thing you remember to do — hosting, monitoring, updates, backups, analytics and a set number of hours of real work every month happen on a schedule, whether or not you thought about it.

The trade is honest and worth stating plainly: you pay every month, and you do not own the build. In exchange, the site is somebody's actual job.

The comparison that matters

Do not compare the subscription prices. Compare these:

  • Whose evenings? A builder is cheaper in cash and more expensive in your time. Price your own hours honestly before you call it the cheap option.
  • What happens in month seven? Be truthful about whether you will still be updating it. Most people will not, and a stale site is worse than a plain one.
  • Who notices when something breaks? On a builder, you. Eventually.
  • What does "done" look like? On a builder, done is when you stop. That is often before the technical basics are finished.

The straight answer

If you will genuinely tend the thing — build it yourself and keep your money. If you know, honestly, that you will not, then a builder is not cheaper. It is a site that gets abandoned at a discount, and the business wearing the consequences is yours.

One more thing nobody says out loud: nobody can honestly promise you Google rankings, on a builder or with us. Anyone who does is selling something they cannot deliver. What is deliverable is a site built so search engines can read it, kept current, and not broken — and that part is work, not magic.
