What you seeLikely causeFirst step

Fuzzy, tufted white growth on a reddened baseEpistylis (often mistaken for ich)Antibacterial approach, not salt
Ragged, receding, or milky finsFin rot (bacterial)Improve water quality first
Cotton-wool white patchesFungusIsolate, antifungal
Swollen belly, scales sticking out ("pinecone")Dropsy (internal, serious)Quarantine; often a late-stage sign

Flashing/scratching with no spotsGill flukes or early parasite / irritationTest water, observe closely

Why the symptom alone isn't enough

Lots of diseases look alike — ich and epistylis are the classic mix-up, and treating one as the other wastes days you don't have. More importantly, most outbreaks start with a water-quality trigger: an ammonia spike, a nitrate climb, a temperature swing, or stress from incompatible tankmates. Treat the disease and ignore the cause and it comes right back.

What to always do first

  • Test the water — ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, pH, temperature. A surprising number of "diseases" are poisoning.
  • Do a water change — it's the safest first move for almost any problem.
  • Quarantine visibly sick fish if you can, before medicating the whole tank.
  • Change one thing at a time and re-check, so you know what worked.
