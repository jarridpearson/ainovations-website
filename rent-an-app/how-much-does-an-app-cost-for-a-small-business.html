How Much Does an App Cost for a Small Business?

Ask an agency to build your business a custom iPhone and Android app and the quote usually lands in the tens of thousands of dollars — before hosting, updates, app-store maintenance, and support are added on. For most small businesses, that math kills the conversation before it starts.

The traditional ways to get an app

  • Custom agency build. The most polished route and the most expensive: a large upfront bill, then ongoing maintenance contracts. Every OS update, store policy change, and bug fix is billable.
  • Freelance developer. Cheaper upfront, but quality varies widely and long-term maintenance is your problem when the freelancer moves on.
  • DIY app builders. Low monthly cost, but you do the work yourself, the result often feels like a template, and you still handle store listings and updates.

All three share the same catch: the app is only the beginning. Hosting, security patches, OS compatibility, and App Store / Google Play compliance never stop. That ongoing burden — not the initial build — is what quietly eats budgets.

The rental alternative

Renting an app flips the model. Instead of paying a five-figure build bill and owning the maintenance burden, you pay a flat monthly rate that covers the build, hosting, updates, and support together. With Rent-an-App, plans start at $299/month on a 1-year agreement — or less per month on a 3-year agreement, where the onboarding fee is waived entirely.

The app itself is a real native iPhone and Android app published under your own developer accounts — your business name on the App Store and Google Play listing. It isn’t a wrapped website or a template you assemble yourself.

Which route fits your business?

If your app is your product — you’re building the next big consumer platform — a custom build may justify its price. But if you want an app that helps customers find you, book you, ask questions, and buy from you, paying tens of thousands upfront rarely makes sense. That’s the case renting was built for.
