Aquatic Frog Tank Setup: Getting African Frogs Right

Fully aquatic African frogs are charming, hardy, and genuinely fun to keep — but there's one mix-up that trips up almost every new keeper at the store. The tiny, peaceful African Dwarf Frog and the much larger, predatory African Clawed Frog look similar as babies and get sold under the same label. Buy the wrong one for a community tank and it will eat your fish. Get the pairing right, and aquatic frogs are a delightful, low-drama addition.

This guide covers how to tell the two apart, the water parameters and setup they need, who they can live with, and how to feed them properly.

First: Dwarf Frog or Clawed Frog?

Before anything else, know which frog you have — it changes everything about the tank.

African Dwarf Frog (ADF). Stays small, around 1.5 inches, peaceful, and spends its life in open water darting to the surface for air. Its front feet are fully webbed. ADFs are community-safe with the right tank mates and live about 5 years.

African Clawed Frog (ACF). Grows to 4–5 inches or more, is a committed predator that will eat any fish or shrimp it can fit in its mouth, and has clawed, unwebbed front toes. ACFs are hardy and can live 15+ years, but they are a species-only animal — keep them alone or with another clawed frog of the same size, never in a community tank.

The tell is the front feet: webbed = dwarf, clawed = clawed frog. Stores mislabel these constantly, so check before you buy.

The Essentials: Warm, Cycled, and Covered

Aquatic frogs are tropical. Both species want water around 72–78°F (22–26°C), so unlike an axolotl they do use a heater. Stable warmth keeps their appetite and immune system healthy.

Your tank must be fully cycled. A frog's permeable skin makes it very sensitive to nitrogenous waste, so your tank needs an established nitrogen cycle with ammonia at 0 ppm and nitrite at 0 ppm before a frog moves in. A fishless cycle takes several weeks — start it ahead of time.

Always keep a lid on. Both ADFs and ACFs will escape a tank without a cover. And because they are air-breathers, don't make the water so deep that the frog struggles to reach the surface.

A solid aquatic frog water parameters target looks like:

  • Temperature: 72–78°F (22–26°C)
  • Ammonia: 0 ppm
  • Nitrite: 0 ppm
  • Nitrate: under ~30 ppm — keep it down with regular partial water changes
  • pH: roughly 6.5–7.8 (stable matters more than a specific number)
  • Flow: gentle — both species are weak swimmers

A few more setup fundamentals:

  • Tank size: about 5–10 gallons works for a small group of dwarf frogs; an African Clawed Frog needs 20+ gallons on its own because it is large and messy.
  • Substrate: use smooth sand or stones too large to swallow. Like axolotls, frogs can ingest small gravel and suffer impaction, so bare-bottom or soft sand is the safe call.
  • Gentle flow: baffle a strong filter output. Dwarf frogs in particular get worn out by heavy current.
  • Hides and plants: caves, driftwood, and live or silk plants give frogs security and resting spots near the surface.

Aquatic Frog Care FAQ

What's the difference between an African Dwarf Frog and an African Clawed Frog?

Size and temperament. A dwarf frog stays tiny (about 1.5 inches), is peaceful, and has fully webbed front feet. A clawed frog grows to 4–5 inches or more, is a predator that eats anything it can fit in its mouth, and has clawed, unwebbed front toes. Check the front feet before you buy — stores mislabel them often.

Can African Dwarf Frogs live with fish?

Yes, with calm, warm-water community fish. Avoid fin-nippers and large or aggressive fish, and make sure the frog actually gets fed, since it's slow and near-sighted. Never house a dwarf frog with a clawed frog.

Do aquatic frogs need a land area?

No. Both species are fully aquatic, but they breathe air and must reach the surface, so don't make the water too deep — and always use a lid, because they escape.

What do aquatic frogs eat?

They're carnivores: sinking frog or carnivore pellets, frozen bloodworms, and brine shrimp. Target-feed dwarf frogs so faster fish don't take all the food.

How warm should an aquatic frog tank be?

Tropical, roughly 72–78°F, fully cycled with ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm.
