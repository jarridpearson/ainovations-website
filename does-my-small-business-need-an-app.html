Does My Small Business Need an App?

Honest answer: most small businesses need a great website first — and only some need an app. An app is not a status symbol; it's a tool for a specific job. If that job isn't there, an app just sits unused on a phone. So before you spend a dime, figure out which side of the line you're on.

You probably DON'T need an app if…

  • Customers mostly find you, check your hours, and call or book once. A fast website does that better — nothing to download.
  • Your goal is to be found on Google. Apps don't show up in search; websites do.
  • You interact with a given customer a few times a year. People won't install an app they open twice.

You might genuinely benefit from an app if…

  • Customers come back often — a gym, a salon, a coffee shop, a service with repeat visits. An icon on the home screen is a standing reminder.
  • You want to send notifications. Push notifications ("your table's ready," "20% off this weekend") are something a website can't do as directly.
  • You have loyalty, memberships, or accounts. Points, punch cards, and logins feel natural in an app.
  • Your staff or crews need a tool in the field — scheduling, checklists, photo uploads. That's often the real win, not the customer-facing side.

The part nobody tells you

The build isn't the expensive part — keeping an app alive is. App Store and Google Play accounts, yearly renewals, OS updates that break things, and the review process every time you change something. That's exactly why a one-time "build me an app" quote often turns into an app that quietly dies a year later. If an app makes sense for you, the smarter move is a managed one — someone owns the stores, the updates, and the upkeep for a flat monthly fee.
