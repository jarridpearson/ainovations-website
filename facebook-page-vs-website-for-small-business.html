Facebook Page vs. Website: Is a Page Enough?

Let's be fair to the Facebook page first: it's free, it's easy, your regulars already follow it, and posting a photo takes ten seconds. For plenty of businesses it's the entire web presence — and it's not nothing.

What a Facebook page quietly costs you

  • Google can barely see it. When someone searches "plumber near me" or "best diner in town," Facebook pages rank far below real websites. The customers who don't already know you can't find you.
  • You don't control it. Reach rules change, pages get locked, and everything you've built sits on rented land with no landlord's number to call.
  • It looks like what it is. To a new customer comparing two businesses, the one with a real website reads as established; the Facebook-only one reads as maybe-still-in-business.
  • Bookings live in your DMs. "Message us for info" means every sale depends on you catching a notification.

The honest answer: keep the page, add the site

Your Facebook page is where your existing customers hang out — keep it. A website is how new customers find you and take you seriously. They're not competitors; they're the two halves of being findable.
