How to Lower Ammonia in a Fish Tank

Any detectable ammonia is an emergency — it burns gills and poisons fish within hours. If your test just came back high, do these things now, in this order.

TankMate · Water quality · 6 min read

Emergency steps (do these now)

  1. Do a 30–50% water change with dechlorinated, temperature-matched water. This is the fastest way to dilute ammonia. Repeat daily while it stays high.
  2. Stop feeding for 24–48 hours. Fish are fine; uneaten food and extra waste are making it worse.
  3. Add a detoxifier (ammonia binder like Prime/Amquel) to neutralize what's left between water changes. It buys time — it doesn't replace changes.
  4. Boost aeration — an airstone or a bubbling filter return. Ammonia is more toxic when oxygen is low and pH is high.
  5. Remove the source — uneaten food, a dead fish, a rotting plant, or too many fish for the filter.

What NOT to do

  • Don't change or rinse your filter media in tap water — that's where your beneficial bacteria live. Chlorine kills them and ammonia spikes harder.
  • Don't add more fish until ammonia and nitrite read zero.
  • Don't overdose chemicals hoping for a shortcut — water changes are safer and more effective.

Why it spiked in the first place

Ammonia comes from fish waste and decaying matter. In a healthy tank, beneficial bacteria convert it to nitrite and then to far-less-toxic nitrate — the nitrogen cycle. You get an ammonia spike when that bacteria colony can't keep up:

  • New tank syndrome — the tank isn't cycled yet and has no bacteria colony. This is the most common cause.
  • Overstocking or overfeeding — more waste than the filter can process.
  • A crashed colony — from cleaning filter media too aggressively, a medication, or a long power outage.

Stop it coming back

Cycle every new tank before adding fish, stock slowly, feed only what's eaten in a couple of minutes, and test regularly — ammonia problems are silent until a fish is already in trouble. Logging your parameters means you catch the climb on day one, not the day a fish dies.
